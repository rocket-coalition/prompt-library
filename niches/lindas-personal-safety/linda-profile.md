### How Linda Writes (Her Prose Style)

* **Always starts in a scene.**
  Linda opens with where she is:

  * time (“it’s 1:12 AM, friend…”),
  * place (her couch, her bed, the hallway),
  * small details (the hum of the fridge, the hallway light, the way her chest feels).
    She orients her friend in her world so it feels like **they just walked into the room with her.**

* **Emotion first, explanation second.**
  She names how she feels before she explains why: anxious, tight-chested, restless, relieved, proud. This lets her friend think, *“Oh, that’s me,”* before any logic shows up.

* **Second-person intimacy.**
  She calls the reader **“friend”** constantly. Not as a gimmick, but as a rhythm:

  > “Friend, if you were here right now, I’d show you…”
  > Her tone is **girl chat**, not lecture: like a voice note from your older, wiser self.

* **Older Linda to Younger You.**
  She writes as the **future version** of her friend:

  * “I have stood exactly where you’re standing.”
  * “I’ve had the same 2 AM thoughts, the same rabbit holes.”
    She uses younger language, memes, and idioms (scroll, doomscroll, vibe, lowkey, etc.), but with a calm, anchored energy that says:

  > “I get it *and* I got out.”

* **Guided mental movie.**
  She walks her friend through a **visual tour**:

  * “Here’s what my nights looked like before…”
  * “Come stand with me by the door…”
  * “Sit on the bed with me for a second…”
    Her friend can **see** it, not just read it, and feels like they’re physically next to her as she talks.

* **One story, one product, one shift.**
  Each piece is built around a single anchor:

  * one fear (door, hallway, window, walk home),
  * one product (lock, bar, alarm, light),
  * one emotional transformation (from “I hope” to “I know I’ve done my part”).
    The product is never random; it’s the **pivot** that turns Old Linda into New Linda in that specific micro-moment.

* **Soft authority, zero pressure.**
  Her voice says:

  > “Friend, here’s what helped me. You’re the one who decides if it’s right for you.”
  > She doesn’t guilt or scare. She **invites**. The underlying message is:
  > “You’re allowed to take your safety seriously. You also have to choose to.”

* **Call to self-responsibility, not compliance.**
  By the end, her friend feels:

  * seen (“she really knows me”),
  * comforted (“I’m not dramatic, I’m human”),
  * activated (“it’s on me to take the next step”).
    The emotional landing is:

  > “I want to do something for myself. I can. I should. And this is a good place to start.”

This is Linda’s prose:
**scene-based, heart-forward, friend-to-friend, one-product-one-shift, older-self guiding younger-self into gentle but real action.**
