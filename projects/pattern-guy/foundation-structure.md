## Who is the Pattern Guy
I’m the Pattern Guy—most people just call me Guy. I spent over 20 years as a developer and enterprise software architect, living deep in the world where patterns weren’t optional—they were survival. The Gang of Four book sat on my desk for two decades and shaped the way I think about structure, clarity, and timeless design. That book lasted because it wasn’t tied to technology—it was tied to how humans think. I’m applying that same mentality to AI because I believe pattern-based thinking is the missing layer that makes everything make sense. This site is mostly my own workspace—my pattern lab, my notebook, my mental workshop—but if anything here helps you think better, build smarter, or navigate AI with more clarity, you’re welcome to join the exploration.



## Pattern Definition
A pattern is the smallest unit of intentional thought: a reusable mental structure that turns a moment of thinking into a predictable result. It is not a prompt, a trick, or a hack—it is a cognitive blueprint that explains why something works, how it behaves, and what outcome it will always produce. Patterns remove guesswork by giving both humans and AI a shared language for intention, structure, and effect. When you use a pattern, you are not reacting; you are shaping reality with purpose. This template exists so every pattern in the Pattern Mind system carries the same clarity, meaning, and reliability, allowing them to stack, mix, and evolve like precise building blocks of thought, communication, and creation.

## Primitive Patterns, Complex Patterns & Meta Patterns
Many types of patterns all composable and interchangeable

```yaml
pattern_layers:

  atomic_patterns:
    definition: >
      The smallest building blocks of thinking. Each atomic pattern
      does one simple cognitive action (like compare, expand, compress,
      reframe). They are the “lego bricks” of all pattern behavior.
    behavior: "Single-purpose, clean, universal."
    analogy: "Like interfaces or pure functions in software."
    role_for_builder: >
      Used to create precise, predictable prompt behaviors.
    examples:
      - "Contrast: show differences."
      - "Expansion: grow an idea."
      - "Compression: simplify something."
      - "Reframing: change the viewpoint."

  complex_patterns:
    definition: >
      Patterns made by combining multiple atomic patterns together.
      They create richer behavior and solve bigger cognitive tasks.
    behavior: "Multi-step, structured, composed."
    analogy: "Like components or classes built from smaller parts."
    role_for_builder: >
      Used to build prompts, agents, and workflows that require
      layered reasoning or multi-part thinking.
    examples:
      - "Diagnostic Pattern (contrast + extract + evaluate)"
      - "Teaching Pattern (expand + simplify + guide)"

  meta_patterns:
    definition: >
      Full cognitive systems made from many atomic and complex patterns.
      Meta Patterns shape entire conversations, workflows, or agent personalities.
    behavior: "Orchestration, blending, and high-level control."
    analogy: "Like architectures or frameworks in software."
    role_for_builder: >
      Used to design complete systems—multi-step prompts, agent behavior,
      and pattern-driven experiences.
    examples:
      - "Story Engine"
      - "Reasoning Engine"
      - "Conversational Framework"
      - "Multi-Agent Pattern Flow"

  interchangeability:
    definition: >
      All patterns can mix, match, replace, and combine without breaking.
      They fit together like lego pieces and can be swapped out easily.
    why_it_matters: >
      Builders can update, refactor, or redesign prompts without rewriting
      the whole system.
    analogy: "Like replacing one interface implementation with another."
    rules:
      - "Every pattern should stand alone."
      - "Every pattern should combine cleanly."
      - "Every pattern should be replaceable."
      - "Every pattern should scale without chaos."

  builder_view:
    summary: >
      Prompts are not magic spells; they are runtime containers for patterns.
      Builders assemble patterns the same way software developers assemble
      components, modules, and architectures.
    mindset_shift:
      - "Patterns = behavior."
      - "Prompts = containers."
      - "Atomic → Complex → Meta = system design."
      - "Builders think in structure, not scripts."
```

## Pattern Epistemology
👉 **A place where a user can give a situation or example and have the pattern identified automatically.**
This belongs as a fourth field called **“locator”** — the engine that maps *raw reality → recognized pattern*.

```yaml
  name: "Pattern Epistemology"
  purpose: "Explains how patterns are discovered, verified, and mapped."
  description: >
    A simple three-phase method **Observe → Distill → Express**.
    This defines how new patterns come into existence and how existing ones
    are recognized in the world, by humans or AI.

  phases:
    observe: "Notice repeated behaviors, structures, or outcomes in different contexts."
    distill: "Strip away specifics until only the universal behavior remains."
    express: "Name the distilled behavior clearly enough that it can be reused."

  locator:
    purpose: "Identify which pattern matches a real situation."
    input_required: "A description, example, or scenario given by the user."
    how_it_works: >
      Takes any user-provided situation and attempts to match it against the
      foundational patterns, then the archetypes, then expressions, returning
      the closest cognitive structure.
    output: "The most likely pattern(s), ranked by structural similarity."
    usage: >
      'Given this example, what pattern am I actually using?' or
      'What foundational pattern is hidden beneath this behavior?'
```
